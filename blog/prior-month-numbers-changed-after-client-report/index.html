What to do when last month's numbers change after you've sent the client report

By Praveen Doddamani · 2026-11-12

You closed the month, built the pack, sent it, and the client said thanks. Three weeks later a supplier bill dated last month turns up, or you spot a reclass that should have been made. Now the profit figure the owner already read is no longer the profit figure in the ledger. This post is a simple, repeatable way to handle that without confusing the client or quietly rewriting history.

It is written for independent bookkeepers working in QuickBooks Online or Xero who send a monthly pack to small business owners. Nothing here replaces your own judgement or any rules your professional body or the client's accountant set; it is a practical default.

Why prior-month changes happen at all

Small businesses rarely have every document in hand by the time you close. Common causes include:

  • Late supplier bills that belong to the previous month.
  • Reclassifications once you learn what a transaction actually was.
  • Bank feed corrections, duplicates or missing transactions found during the next reconciliation.
  • Accruals or prepayments agreed after the fact with the client or their accountant.
  • Client answers to questions you parked as uncategorised.

None of these mean the close was careless. They are a normal side effect of reporting quickly. The problem is not the change; it is a change the client discovers on their own.

Decide on a materiality line before it happens

The worst time to decide whether a change matters is when you are looking at it. Set a rule per client, in plain words, and write it in your notes. For example: "If a change moves last month's net profit or cash position by more than an amount the owner would act on, I mention it. Otherwise I post it and it flows into the comparatives."

What counts as material depends on the business. A small amount can matter for a client running close to break-even, and the same amount can be noise for a busier one. The point is to choose once, not to agonise every month.

Two clean options: post in the current month, or restate

Once you know a change is real, you broadly have two ways to treat it in the report.

Option 1: Book it in the current month

For small or routine items, post the adjustment in the month you found it and leave the prior month as reported. This keeps the numbers the client already saw stable. In the commentary, add one line if it distorts the current month: "Includes a late October supplier bill of X, so November costs look slightly higher than usual."

Option 2: Correct the prior month and restate

For material items, or anything the client's accountant wants in the right period, correct the transaction date in the ledger. Your software will then show a different figure for last month the next time you run a report. That is fine, as long as you say so. Silent restatement is what erodes trust: the owner compares two packs, sees different numbers for the same month, and wonders which one to believe.

If the period is locked in QuickBooks Online or Xero, follow your normal process for unlocking and re-locking, and keep a short note of what changed and why.

How to show a restatement in the monthly pack

Keep it simple and visible. A few habits that help:

  • Label the comparison column. If last month is restated, mark it "October (restated)" rather than just "October."
  • Add a short reconciliation. One small table: previously reported net profit, the adjustment, restated net profit. Three lines is usually enough.
  • Explain in the commentary, not a footnote. One or two sentences near the top: what changed, why, and whether it affects any decision the owner made.
  • Keep trend charts consistent. If you show a six-month trend, use the restated figure so the chart matches the ledger. The label and commentary do the explaining.
  • Keep the original pack. Save the PDF you sent. If anyone asks later what the owner was told, you have it.

How to tell the client

Most of the time, the next monthly pack is the right place. If the change is large enough that the owner might act differently, for instance on a tax set-aside, a loan conversation or a hiring decision, send a short note straight away rather than waiting.

A plain example you can adapt:

"Quick heads-up: a supplier bill dated October arrived after I sent your October pack. I've moved it into October so the month is correct. Net profit for October is now lower than the figure in the pack. Nothing else changes, and November's pack will show October as restated with a short note. Happy to walk through it if useful."

Notice what it does: it states the cause, the direction of the change, what happens next, and offers a conversation. It does not over-apologise or bury the number. If you already send a covering email with each pack, this fits naturally into it; our post on writing the month-end email to bookkeeping clients covers that format.

Reduce how often it happens

You cannot remove late information, but you can shrink it:

  • Send a short "anything outstanding?" request a few days before month end.
  • Keep a running list of open questions and clear it before building the pack.
  • Review unpaid bills and recurring suppliers for anything missing.
  • Agree with each client how long you wait for documents before closing.

A consistent close routine makes all of this easier. If you want a starting point, see the month-end close checklist for bookkeepers.

The habit that matters most

Clients do not expect every number to be final the moment it lands. They do expect not to be surprised. Pick a materiality line, choose between posting forward and restating, label restated figures, and say so in plain words. Done consistently, a prior-month change becomes evidence that you are watching the books closely rather than a reason to doubt them.
