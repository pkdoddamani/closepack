How to get clients to answer uncategorized transaction questions before month-end

By Praveen Doddamani · 2026-11-09

Most month-end delays in a small bookkeeping practice are not caused by the bookkeeping. They are caused by a short list of transactions nobody can explain without the client. A card payment to an unfamiliar vendor, a transfer between accounts, a deposit with no invoice behind it. Until someone answers, the books are not quite closed and the monthly report waits.

This post is about the client side of that problem: how to ask about uncategorized transactions in a way that gets answers, what to do when the answers do not come, and how to keep the month moving either way. None of it needs special software. It is mostly about how the questions are written and when they are sent.

Why the question list stalls in the first place

From the client's side, a message that says "can you look at these transactions?" is a vague task with no end. They have to open their bank app, remember what they bought weeks ago, and work out what you actually need to know. It is easy to put off, and once it is put off it tends to stay put off.

A few habits on the bookkeeper's side make this worse without meaning to: questions sent one at a time as they come up, questions buried in a long email about something else, questions that ask the client to log in somewhere, and questions with no clear deadline. Each one adds a small amount of friction, and friction is what turns a five-minute job into a two-week wait.

Batch the questions once, at a predictable time

The simplest change is to stop sending questions ad hoc. Do your bank feed and reconciliation work, collect every open item into one list, and send it once at the same point each month. Clients learn that the question email arrives, say, a few working days after month-end, and that answering it is the last thing standing between them and their report.

If you already work from a close checklist, the question list belongs as an explicit step in it, right after reconciliations and before you draft the report. Our month-end close checklist for bookkeepers covers where it fits in the wider sequence.

Make each question answerable in one line

The goal is that a client can answer every question from their phone without opening anything else. For each transaction, give them what they need to recognise it:

  • The date, the amount and the description exactly as it appears on their statement.
  • Which account or card it came from.
  • Your best guess, if you have one. "Looks like software, is this for the business?" is far easier to answer than "what is this?"
  • A short list of likely options where it helps, such as personal, equipment, or stock.

Offering a guess matters more than it seems. Most clients will happily confirm or correct a suggestion. Very few will volunteer a full explanation from scratch.

Number the questions so the client can reply with "1 yes, 2 personal, 3 not sure" and be done. Keep the list in the body of the email rather than in an attachment, for the same reason the rest of your month-end email should be readable without opening anything; we cover that in more detail in how to write the month-end email to bookkeeping clients.

Separate what blocks the close from what can wait

Not every open item is equally important. A large unexplained deposit might change revenue for the month. A small coffee shop charge probably does not. Splitting the list into two short sections helps the client prioritise:

  • Needed before I can finish this month: items large enough, or unusual enough, to change the numbers the client will read.
  • When you have a moment: small or routine items you can park and tidy up next month.

This also protects you. If the important items are answered and the small ones are not, you can still close the month with confidence and note the remainder.

Set a cut-off and say what happens after it

Questions without a date tend to drift. Give a clear cut-off and explain plainly what you will do if there is no reply. For example: "If I have not heard back by Thursday, I will post items 4 to 6 to a suspense account and send your report as normal. We can move them next month."

That sentence does two things. It tells the client the report is not hostage to their inbox, and it makes the cost of not replying visible without being pushy. Many bookkeepers find that a calm, specific default gets more replies than repeated reminders.

Whatever you choose, keep it consistent. If the default is a suspense or "ask my accountant" account, use the same one every month so it is easy to review and clear.

Follow up once, then move on

A single short reminder the day before the cut-off is usually enough. Reply in the same thread so the list is right there, and repeat only the blocking items. After the cut-off, apply your default, close the month and send the report.

In the report itself, it is worth a short line in your commentary noting how many items were posted to suspense and roughly what they total. That keeps the numbers honest and gives the client a gentle reason to answer next time. Your commentary is the right place for that kind of judgement, which is why we think it should stay written and owned by the bookkeeper.

Reduce the list over time

The best question list is a shorter one. Each month, look at what you had to ask about and whether a rule could have handled it:

  • Recurring vendors that can get a bank rule in QuickBooks Online or Xero.
  • Personal spending on a business card that might be better on a separate card, if the client is willing.
  • Receipts that could be captured at the time of purchase with whatever receipt app the client already uses.

Raise one of these at a time, ideally in the same email as the report, when the client is already thinking about their numbers. Small changes compound, and a client who gets three questions a month instead of fifteen is far more likely to answer quickly.

A short template you can adapt

Hi [Name],

I have finished the bank and card reconciliations for [Month]. There are a few transactions I need you to confirm before I can finish your report. A one-line reply is perfect, for example "1 yes, 2 personal".

Needed before I finish this month
1. [Date] · [Amount] · [Statement description] · [Account]. Looks like [guess], is that right?
2. ...

When you have a moment
3. ...

If I have not heard back by [day], I will post anything still open to [suspense account] and send your report as normal. We can tidy those up next month.

Thanks,
[Your name]

Edit the wording so it sounds like you. The structure is what does the work: one list, easy answers, a clear default and a date.
